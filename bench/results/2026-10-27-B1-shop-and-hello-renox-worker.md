Verdict: **PROVISIONAL FAIL** for the F1 gate condition (#37): `shop` costs 22.96 MB RSS per warm isolate at N = 100 (PSS 22.96 MB, V8 heap 13.9 MB), over the 20 MB limit. `hello` passes at 7.5 MB per isolate (F0 Edge Runtime baseline: 9.77 MB). Measured on a 4 vCPU / 16 GB cloud container, not `node-small`.

## F1 findings

- **Not the eszip form yet.** The CLI has no eszip step (#30/#32 left it out), so the `shop` artifact is `esm-dir`: every function module is bundled with its own copy of its dependencies and an inline source map. zod is in both `cart.js` and `checkout.js`; `checkout.js` alone is 3.2 MB (Stripe + source map), `cart.js` 0.9 MB. All five modules are evaluated at isolate creation.
- The V8 heap per `shop` isolate is 13.9 MB against 4.2 MB for `hello`: about 9.7 MB of heap is the evaluated module code and closures of zod + Stripe (twice for zod). The rest of the RSS delta (≈ 9 MB) is the isolate's non-heap memory (code space, thread stacks of the isolate and watchdog threads, allocator arenas).
- The gate condition as written (B1 ≤ 20 MB as eszip) cannot be judged until the CLI emits eszip. What this run says: in the form the platform deploys today, `shop` is over the limit by 15%. Expected levers: one shared vendor chunk instead of per-module copies (needs `esm-dir` chunks or eszip), no inline source maps in the deployed artifact (upload them separately, as #128 plans for client maps), and lazy evaluation of modules not needed by the first request.
- The per-isolate cost falls with N (shop 37.9 → 23.0 MB from N = 1 to 100) because process-wide pages (lazy extension sources, allocator arenas) are paid once; N = 100 is the closest to steady state.
