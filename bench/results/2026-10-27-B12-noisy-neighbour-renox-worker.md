## F1 findings

- **Isolation holds.** Tenant A's 50 req/s of `/api/loop` and `/api/alloc` burn up to 2.5 CPU-seconds per second in theory (each request may use 50 ms) and recycle A's isolate on every termination, yet B's latency does not move: A's work runs on A's isolate thread, the CPU watchdog stops it, and B's isolate thread is never blocked. B's throughput was slightly higher with A running (frequency/scheduling noise, not a real gain).
- **Termination timing.** 2,241 requests ran into the limit themselves; their charged `cpu_ms` has median 51.3 ms, p99 60.8 ms, max 131 ms. The tail comes from `/api/alloc`: a terminate request is honoured only at V8's next interrupt check, and a large allocation that triggers a full GC runs to completion first. The contract allows over-counting (`ops.md` section 10: limits fire early, never late); termination before 2× the limit holds for p99 but not for the max. Follow-up issue opened.
- **Collateral terminations.** 6,762 of A's 9,003 `cpu_limit` records were requests that happened to be in flight on the isolate when another request was terminated (`ops.md` section 10: every in-flight request on a poisoned isolate ends with the same code). At this rate almost every A request fails; that is the intended blast radius (A's own deployment), but it means one bad route takes down a deployment's concurrent good requests. Worth stating in the tenant docs.
- `/api/alloc` never reached the 128 MB heap limit: the 50 ms CPU limit stops it first (4,500 × `cpu_limit`, 0 × `oom`). The heap limit path is covered by `crates/renox-runtime/tests/termination.rs` and `limits.rs`.
